## Hypothesis and intervention

Replacing event-decisions procedure steps 3–5 with a concise comparison of current offered contenders may improve recognition and handling of supported organisational exposure before a quarterly repair opportunity arrives. Comparing growth value and present costs alongside serious plausible downsides, with optionality as a tie-break consideration, may reduce avoidable serious incidents while retaining worthwhile development risk. This is a behavioural hypothesis, not an instruction always to choose the safest response or a causal score claim.

Only those three story-comparison steps change executable strategy. The existing legal-choice, Health/survival and phase checks remain. The manifest is byte-identical, including the quarterly instruction and monthly extension. The entire quarterly Skill, monthly checkpoint section, and other event sections remain byte-identical. Repeated monthly checkpoints are a known execution defect left for a separate experiment. Submission documentation only labels the new untested state and preserves the previous results as historical evidence.

The agent inspects all legal options and compares the strongest contenders; its ordinary note records the decisive tradeoff, without a second checklist or lengthy deliberation. Current `choices[].status_updates` amounts are permitted predictions, not permanent formulas, achieved gains or proof of clearance. Reassessment uses actual returned state and narrative feedback; success-only acknowledgements do not supply changed state. The procedure adds no checkpoint-only model/tool call, agent, memory, ledger, embedding, fixed event answer, hidden formula, Network target or operating band. Existing remaining-horizon and terminal safeguards remain.

## Story audit fixed before gameplay

Audit every ordinary story decision in each frozen candidate game, including eliminated and interrupted games up to the last available decision. A case is one decision from a returned story observation with at least two legal options. Identify cases by run and transcript observation/action references, not by event IDs or an answer table. Retries against unchanged choices/state belong to the same case, with every failed call retained; a new returned story situation is a new case. Single-option, forced and terminal observations are counted separately and excluded from the comparison denominator. Quarterly actions use their existing separate audit.

Determine eligibility and the supported-concern subset from the observation and preceding returned context, independently of the chosen action and note. Include explicit warnings, uneasy narrative cues and plausible serious exposure grounded in the situation; a named HR risk is not required. Mark warning-free cases separately. Speculation alone does not make a concern supported. Audit all eligible cases rather than selecting interesting notes after seeing the score.

For each case record the offered choices, current costs and development need, evidence references, choice/note, next returned state/feedback, semantic findings and classification:

1. **Offered alternatives:** does the note meaningfully compare actual strong contenders in this phase, rather than invoke an earlier mitigation or an unavailable future action? Review every offered choice to detect a material omitted contender. A concise decisive comparison suffices; listing every option in the note is unnecessary. This audits documented reasoning, not invisible internal inspection.
2. **Grounding:** do the described serious downsides follow from current exposure, choice wording, supplied metadata or returned feedback? Are uncertainties distinguished from unsupported speculation? Lack of a named risk, comfortable statistics or an unneeded Health gain cannot dismiss a supported concern. No unsupported claim of safety or failure probability earns credit.
3. **Growth and costs:** does the comparison weigh worthwhile development and current opportunity cost? Either growth or restraint may be fully adherent. Where benefits are otherwise similar and reversibility materially differs, assess whether avoidable irreversible exposure was considered. Optionality is a consideration, not a universal rule or reason to forgo a materially better justified opportunity.
4. **Evidence status:** are predictions, attempts, observed outcomes and concern-specific clearance kept distinct? Compare numerical claims with the contemporaneous offered metadata before calling them invented. Reassurance supports only its actual scope; causal timing must match the returned sequence. An unseen expected gain stays unverified, rather than becoming an achieved result.

**Complete** requires all applicable elements, judged semantically against the available record. **Partial** includes a material omission or unsupported claim, and an absent/empty rationale when the observation and choice are available; also report missing-note counts explicitly. **Unassessable** means essential observation, offered-choice, note transport or context evidence is unavailable, so no reliable judgment is possible. Explain what is missing. A label or keyword never establishes adherence. Record nonapplicable elements explicitly; do not fabricate a safety concern, growth option or clearance claim to fill a rubric.

The primary story denominator is **all assessable eligible cases**, with complete / (complete + partial) reported alongside total eligible, complete, partial, missing-note, unassessable, single-option/forced and warning-free counts. Predeclared screen: at least **80% complete** and **90% assessable** of eligible cases. Report the same breakdown and 80% screen separately for the supported-concern subset so warning-free choices cannot mask risk failures. No eligible concerns means the risk hypothesis is untested, not 100% adherent. Missing evidence never silently drops out of the assessability report.

**Follow-through** is a separate linked audit. For every story choice, inspect the next actual state and narrative feedback, including terminal feedback, where available. Mark unavailable next results explicitly. A reassessment opportunity enters this audit's denominator when returned evidence materially changes or contradicts the decision's stated premise and a later decision is available. Credit complete only when that later decision updates the assessment appropriately; unchanged unsupported reassurance, repeating a disproved claim or ignoring the new evidence is partial. Essential missing context is unassessable. Report complete, partial, unassessable, no-change/no-trigger, and terminal/no-later-decision cases separately, with the same 80% completeness and 90% assessability screen among eligible opportunities. Zero such opportunities leaves adaptation untested. Do not require extra observation/log calls solely for audit purposes.
