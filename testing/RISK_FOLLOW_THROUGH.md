## Hypothesis and scope

Replacing the existing quarterly warning reminder with a short comparison in the ordinary decision note may improve documented risk follow-through. The note connects current unresolved evidence, legal mitigation versus routine growth, the selected action's opportunity cost, and evidence that would justify reconsideration. This is an execution hypothesis, not a claim that more mitigation always improves scores.

Only the manifest's existing warning sentence and quarterly procedure steps 2 and 5 change strategy. The event Skill and the manifest's monthly extension remain byte-identical to the latest candidate. No operating bands, Network targets, token redesign, new ledger, memory mechanism, event answers or inferred formulas are introduced. The monthly focus remains revisable whenever evidence or danger changes; it is not an exclusive task. Missing history cannot block decisions and no new formal review this month is normal.

The first ordinary Energy decision and the main-action decision each expose the comparison. Later Energy notes update it when evidence, affordability or competing needs change. There is no additional action or lookup solely to fill a note. When no concern is supported, the agent can say that. A serious warning normally outweighs marginal growth, but an urgent Health or other survival need can displace mitigation with an explanation. Current legal actions and published directions govern; no particular mitigation is compulsory each quarter. One attempt, a reassuring statistic or improved HR feedback cannot establish blanket clearance.

## Semantic audit

For every first Energy and main-action decision, link the returned observation, available warning context, legal choices and note. Judge whether the concern is supported, the mitigation is currently legal/affordable, growth and opportunity cost are compared, the chosen action is justified now, and the proposed subsequent evidence addresses that concern. An action name or four labels alone earns no full credit. A defensible survival override can be fully adherent without mitigation. Unsupported blanket clearance fails the relevant element. Audit later Energy decisions when evidence or affordability changes; distinguish legitimate concise carry-forward from stale reasoning.

Record mitigation attempts and later evidence separately. Unchanged visible statistics mean no visible change, not automatically unavailable evidence or proof of no possible benefit. Improved HR feedback may support reassessment of its scope without clearing unrelated concerns. Repetition is valid when current evidence and opportunity cost justify it; neither variety nor a mitigation quota earns credit.

Retain the separate monthly audit: month, first story note reference, prior focus, observed improvement/no visible change/unavailable evidence, latest available formal review, current focus, quarterly/story carry-forward and justified revision. Mark partial and unassessable cases explicitly. Use actual notes and feedback, not keywords or presumed hidden reasoning.

Also audit surviving safeguards: current phase and affordability; fresh returned state before state-dependent spending; observation when an action only acknowledges success; no predicted stat-gain spending chains; restraint when Health declines; no guessed thresholds or cross-stat raw rankings; no promotion/review beyond the remaining horizon; no action after an authoritative ending. These are retained requirements, not simultaneous experimental strategy changes.
