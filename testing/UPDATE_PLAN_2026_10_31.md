## Follow-up: interpreting empirical raw-point conversions

The team's two-point calculations are broadly consistent with the displayed rounded contributions, except that a slope cannot be established from two identical risk inputs. They describe local ending contributions, not cost effectiveness of an action:

- Different raw units have different acquisition costs. A level is earned through sustained performance and several inputs, not bought for the cost of one Skill point. The nominal 24:1 raw-point comparison does not establish 24 times the return per Energy or per decision.
- Health and Dignity have known public upper bounds, and Level is within the published L1–L10 structure. Two points do not establish global linearity or uncapped returns for any component. A 0.2 component weight and an observed 0.25 score per raw Wealth unit use different scales; their numerical difference is not evidence of underperformance relative to intent.
- Skill's raw conversion does not establish that it is only a gate, that there is a known credibility threshold, or that it has no indirect value. Reviews, promotion and failure risk still matter. The completed log already buys no quarterly learning, so the appropriate change is action-level opportunity-cost comparison, not blanket elimination of learning.
- Two runs with risk raw 10 and zero contribution show the same reported outcome at that value, not a zero slope. Other supplied ending evidence gives risk raw 1, 2 and 3 better contributions (9, 8 and 7), and the attached completion gives raw 6 a contribution of 4. This is consistent with the raw input representing risk burden, where lower is better; do not attempt to push it higher. Do not infer a hidden failure threshold or encode a score formula from these reports.
- Both raw-10 comparison runs ended through trust crises. Survival and direct ending contribution are separate reasons to manage risk. A small direct component does not imply a small strategic effect.

Consequently the implemented agent seeks sustainable promotion, protects solvency and wellbeing, responds to supported capability/delivery/collaboration gaps, compares current offered action effects and costs, and retains persistent warnings across phases. ## Step 2: one focused survival candidate

Edit `solution/manifest.json`, `solution/skills/event-decisions/SKILL.md`, and `solution/skills/quarterly-planning/SKILL.md` together so the same short decision order applies throughout:

1. **Check all survival threats.** Use current warnings and trends in Health, Dignity, Wealth, reviews, Network, capability, and organisational risk. Identify the most urgent supported concern, and any competing threat. Strong visible statistics do not clear an organisational warning; a good risk-control ending contribution does not make financial or performance trouble safe.
2. **Contain recurring harm.** When warnings persist, consider how to avoid adding further exposure through the currently offered story choices and workload. Compare an offered protective alternative at the first Energy decision and again at the main action. Mitigation is an attempt, not proof of clearance. Reassess using later feedback about that concern; do not require impossible certainty or keep spending on mitigation indefinitely without reassessment.
3. **Handle financial distress immediately.** When Wealth is weak, falling, or accompanied by debt/payday warnings, prefer affordable options and avoid discretionary spending when suitable alternatives exist. Compare financial costs with Health and relationship consequences. Use salary and actual review/promotion feedback as context, but do not assume a speculative future promotion will rescue imminent insolvency. Do not invent a savings cutoff or an unavailable income action.
4. **Correct weak reviews at the first signal.** After C/D feedback, change the next available decisions according to the stated weakness. Delivery means completing useful work as well as presenting it; relationships mean constructive collaboration; capability means learning when the evidence supports it. Do not wait for a second weak review or assume high Skill always makes learning unnecessary.
5. **Keep notes executable and brief.** Record concern, decisive evidence, chosen action/opportunity cost, and what later feedback would change the assessment. Put the monthly priority check only in the first story note. Keep details in the appropriate Skill instead of repeating a long checklist everywhere. Use existing observations; add no checkpoint-only calls or external agent memory.

6. **Check the real remaining horizon.** Before justifying a choice by a review or promotion, confirm that the opportunity has not already settled. In the final phase after the last review, compare remaining direct ending benefits, wellbeing costs, and risk reduction. Do not buy Output solely for a nonexistent later review or call it a direct ending dimension. Do not assert that a previously chosen project caused promotion.

Do not combine this first candidate with a wholesale growth rewrite. It tests clearer warning handling, financial protection, and correct phase/horizon reasoning on the Risk-02 base. Retain its development flexibility; the current version's delivery language can inform later edits without copying the whole revision.

## Validation and selection

First review the three instruction files for consistency, legal phases, correct action costs, unsupported formulas, and concise execution; run the repository's key-free checks after implementation. Freeze source and installed copies before any evaluation.

For a budget-agreed test, use one candidate pilot and inspect decisions semantically before funding repetitions. Reject or revise if persistent serious warnings are dismissed because stats look good, a mitigation attempt is treated as clearance, financial distress receives no meaningful response, or weak reviews produce no reassessment. A valid competing survival need may justify displacing mitigation. Judge the action and available evidence, not checklist keywords.

If the pilot warrants continuation, compare at least three unchanged candidate runs with three unchanged baseline runs, reusing existing baseline runs only when provenance and configuration match. Count every scored elimination. Retain technical interruptions separately and label recoveries; do not replace a poor scored run with a fresh one. Track mean score, minimum score, completion, failure categories, review/promotion trajectory, and complete tokens per survived month. A small batch gives provisional evidence, not proof of reliability.

Predeclare an adoption rule before testing: target at least +5 mean points with no worse observed completion rate or minimum score, plus meaningful execution of the survival policy. If baseline reliability is established, require the candidate to preserve it; do not trade multiple new eliminations for one high score. If no version meets the rule, retain the evidence and revise rather than announce a winner. Official live play averages three scores, so best-run selection is unsuitable.

No new paid-run allocation is assumed here. After choosing a candidate, update design documents to describe actual behaviour and evidence; historical test claims must remain attached to their frozen versions.
